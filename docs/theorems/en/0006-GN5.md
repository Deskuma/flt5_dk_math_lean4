# 0006 — `GN5`

> This document is the English translation corresponding to the Japanese canonical edition.

## Lean type

```lean
def GN5 (g y : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  g ^ 4
    + 5 * g ^ 3 * y
    + 10 * g ^ 2 * y ^ 2
    + 10 * g * y ^ 3
    + 5 * y ^ 4
```

Its fully qualified name is `DkMath.FLT.Five.GN5`.

## Mathematical statement

`GN5 g y` is the homogeneous degree-four polynomial left after extracting the gap factor `g` from the fifth-power difference

$$
(g+y)^5-y^5.
$$

Thus,

$$
(g+y)^5-y^5=g\,GN5(g,y).
$$

Equivalently, `GN5` is the gap-coordinate form obtained by substituting `z=g+y` into the standard fifth cyclotomic factor

$$
z^4+z^3y+z^2y^2+zy^3+y^4.
$$

## Role in the complete proof

The previous articles established the positive gap `z-y`. This definition fixes that gap as the local coordinate `g` and packages the residual factor required to rewrite the fifth-power difference as

$$
g\,GN5(g,y).
$$

Later theorems decompose `GN5` in two directions:

- modulo the gap `g`;
- modulo the prime `5`.

Those two congruence views control common prime divisors of the gap and the residual factor, explaining why `5` is the exceptional prime in the primitive route.

## Direct dependencies

There are no direct project-specific dependencies. The definition uses only:

- natural numbers `ℕ`;
- addition and multiplication;
- natural-number powers;
- the numerals `4`, `5`, and `10`.

Its mathematical motivation, however, comes from the earlier positive gap `g=z-y` and `fifth_sub_eq_of_add_eq`.

## Construction flow

The coefficients arise from the binomial expansion

$$
(g+y)^5=g^5+5g^4y+10g^3y^2+10g^2y^3+5gy^4+y^5.
$$

After subtracting `y^5` and factoring out `g`, one obtains

$$
(g+y)^5-y^5=g\left(g^4+5g^3y+10g^2y^2+10gy^3+5y^4\right).
$$

The expression in parentheses is exactly `GN5 g y`.

## Lean-specific processing

This declaration is a computable `def`, not a theorem. Later polynomial identities can therefore use

```lean
unfold GN5
ring
```

so that `ring` performs polynomial normalization.

The definition contains no natural-number subtraction. This is an important design advantage: truncated subtraction is handled by outer lemmas together with order hypotheses, while `GN5` remains a positive-coefficient polynomial using only addition, multiplication, and powers.

## Redundancy and duplication

`GN5` contains the same mathematical information as the standard cyclotomic factor after substituting `z=g+y`. The two forms nevertheless have different purposes:

- the standard form displays the general factorization of a fifth-power difference;
- the gap form makes congruence arguments in `g` and five-adic analysis direct.

The duplication is therefore best understood as an intentional change of coordinates.

## Optimization candidates

The definition is short and is not itself a performance target. Possible API-level directions include:

- deriving it as the `d=5` specialization of a general gap polynomial;
- organizing the standard-factor correspondence as a rewrite API;
- auditing whether the gap and five-adic decomposition lemmas should receive `[simp]` attributes.

A general abstraction would also increase proof and maintenance cost. For an FLT5-specific module, the current concrete polynomial may be easier to normalize and audit. This is a design assessment, not a source-level fact.

## Mathlib imports and import optimization

The standalone file uses `import Mathlib`, under which both the definition and the following `ring` proofs are available.

The definition alone likely needs only small algebraic imports for naturals, multiplication, addition, and powers. The surrounding module additionally needs the `ring` tactic. A useful audit should therefore distinguish the minimal import for the definition from the minimal import for the complete `GN5.lean` module. Exact minimal imports were not verified in this article.

## Next declaration

The next declaration is `DkMath.FLT.Five.GN5_eq_homogeneous_cyclotomic`.

It proves that the newly defined gap polynomial equals the standard fifth cyclotomic factor

$$
(g+y)^4+(g+y)^3y+(g+y)^2y^2+(g+y)y^3+y^4.
$$

This formally establishes that `GN5` is not an unrelated auxiliary polynomial but exactly the standard residual factor of the fifth-power difference.
